# Generated Figures Follow Explicit Scene Staging

A ScenePlan's `characterIds` are an initial cast hint, not the visible cast or a hard whitelist. Dialogist owns beat-level Scene Staging and emits explicit `show` and `hide` Figure Cues with a stable project `characterId`; every `show` also selects `left`, `center`, or `right` and an English emotion tag. A cue for another known project Character extends the Scene cast when the performance requires it, while unknown Characters remain invalid. Several characters may remain visible at once, characters may enter or leave without speaking, and no rule maps the first two participants to fixed sides or puts every participant on screen. The deterministic local fallback uses the same staging schema instead of reverting to dialogue-driven automatic binding.

Each visible character/emotion pair owns a Figure asset task and character sprite entry. SceneScript compiles a `show` to `changeFigure:none` preceded by that task's marker, so playable content does not reference a file that has not been promoted; `hide` compiles directly to `changeFigure:none`. After promotion, the binder resolves only matching task markers, preserves the stable Character ID, and replaces the placeholder with the promoted filename. Scenes without the staging marker remain on the legacy dialogue-based binding path for backward compatibility.

Configured Figure generation always runs the local BiRefNet matting step and emits PNG. The common promotion boundary, including manual Artifact promotion and recovery-time rebind, rejects non-PNG files and PNGs with no transparent pixels; matting failure may only downgrade to an explicitly transparent placeholder, never the opaque generated source. On same-run recovery, the latest StoryPlan and compiled Scenes are authoritative: only tasks whose generation and binding inputs are unchanged retain prior success, while added or changed Figure variants become pending and removed tasks are not rebound.

WebGAL's left, center, and right positions are the initial target-engine vocabulary. Arbitrary transforms, depth, animation, camera framing, and per-layer occlusion require a later cue-schema extension; they are not inferred by the binder.
